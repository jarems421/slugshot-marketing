# Format: In My World / In This Town

Evidence: 631 TikTok posts (26 Nov 2026), tiers TOP / MID / FLOP of 50 each, with Gemini's codes checked by two blind coders. Numbers are in evidence.md; the ledger is `IMW-GEMINI-VERIFY`. These findings apply to this format only.

## CONFIRMED (association, survived blind checks)

1. **Original, never a copy or reskin.** No TOP post copied an earlier one; 3 MID and 6 FLOP did, often copying hits from other creators. Noun-swapping someone's beat sheet ("burger" → "cow") is a reskin. The checker compares drafts with the 631 posts.
2. **A concrete, everyday rule. No stock fantasy, ranks or power levels as the premise.** Generic fantasy leans towards flops under both blind coders (TOP 3–6 vs FLOP 13–14 of 50), and power/rank premises the same way. Slugs, arenas and partners are fine. A straight "F-to-S rank" world isn't. (Powerscaling as humour in other formats isn't covered by this.)
3. **Specific, voiced narration, not generic AI prose.** TOP 1 vs FLOP 11 where both blind coders agree. See voice-and-dialogue.md.
4. **The caption states the premise** ("In this town, burgers are illegal"), not "Part 3" or "In this town…". TOP 43 vs FLOP 32 of 50.

## CONVENTIONS (every tier does these; not performance advantages)

- An "In my world / In this town, [rule]." line early (45–46 of 50 in every tier).
- Brisk narration of about 210 wpm (TOP 218, MID 221, FLOP 231 median). Our V06 at about 100 wpm was outside the genre's range.
- Narration from the first frame; speech covers about 90% of runtime; a steady music bed under the voice.
- Length mostly 60–170 s (TOP median 109 s; none over 240 s reached TOP).

Use them so the video reads as the format. Break one on purpose when it helps, for example a slow deadpan read as the joke, or a cold open with the rule as a reveal. Note the break in the handoff.

## Moves the genre often uses (a menu, not a template)

Rule line · "you see" explanation · townsfolk examples (often three) · narrator's name and quirk · inciting turn about a third in · escalation · twist · button.

**Don't make every script the same shape.** In the 150 tier transcripts, "you see" appears in 37 and "except me" in 25, and the same few names keep recurring (Max and Leo in 17 each, Mia in 12, Theo in 11). That sameness is the average we're trying to beat. Vary how the world is shown (one vivid example can beat three), who tells it, and where the turn falls.

## Cautions (small numbers, consistent)

- **Numbered sequels** lost 3–18x reach. Prefer standalone stories in a shared world with recurring characters. A Part 2 needs a short recap of the rule.
- **YouTube-style "subscribe" lines and coercive bait** ("subscribe to get your real mom") appear only in MID and FLOP. A real choice question is fine.

## TEST (modest or unverified; write variants)

- A fresh premise helps modestly (blind novelty 3.5 TOP vs 3.0 FLOP). Some comic edge helps modestly.
- Deadpan as *the* winning style and "flat endings flop" did not replicate blind.
- A plain hidden-power save leans slightly towards flops; prefer a reframing payoff.
- Gemini's six formula routes (formulas.md) are templates to try.
- Breaking out in the under-5-day posts on 26 Nov: fourth-wall / platform-as-physics premises and language-rule comedies. One snapshot only.
