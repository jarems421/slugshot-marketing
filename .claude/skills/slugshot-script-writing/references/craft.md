# Craft toolbox (JUDGEMENT: options, not steps)

Pick what serves the idea. Nothing here is required.

## Premise engines (use several when diverging)

- **Literalise an everyday thing:** a saying, a habit, a feeling or a platform behaviour made physical ("your screen time controls the weather"; "every lie costs your family $100").
- **One rule plus its cost:** a power, a law or a gift that comes with a catch the story will collect on.
- **Flip a known situation:** the normal order reversed (babies make the rules) or the POV moved (the basketball narrates).
- **Status made visible:** a number, a colour, a weight or a height that shows something people usually hide.
- **Slug-native:** a premise that only works in our world. Partners chosen at hatching, slugs shot from blasters, glowing caverns, a starter slug nobody wants. Strongest for marketing, because the world *is* the joke.
- **Character-first:** start from a cast trait (Floppy's dead-serious dignity, Rammstone's fragile ego, Aquabeek saying one word) and build the situation that tests it.
- **Collision:** put two unrelated ideas together (a heist plus nap time, a nature documentary plus a bottle cap).
- **Honest observation:** something true about being a kid, a gamer or a friend, exaggerated one notch.

Quick filter: could a stranger picture it in about 4 seconds? Is it ours, not a reskin? Can you already see a payoff?

## Hooks (the first seconds)

- **Honest promise:** the opening sells exactly what the payoff delivers. An over-promising hook loses viewers mid-video. *(C: adapted from short-form-video-script, MIT)*
- **Layered:** line, picture and on-screen text all pointing at the same idea, with the most striking frame first and no intro. It should still make sense muted: captions plus picture carry the premise.
- **Hook types to try:**
  - the rule itself;
  - a consequence before the cause ("My dad has three chipped teeth");
  - a character in an absurd position;
  - a question the viewer can't resist;
  - a contradiction ("the weakest slug in the arena, and the only one smiling").
- Write 2–3 openings and choose in critique. Don't open every video the same way, even if a format convention suggests it.

## Progression moves (combine freely; the order is yours)

Establish the world through examples · introduce the narrator or protagonist · complication · attempt · worse complication · reveal · reversal · false relief · ticking clock · clue chain · callback · button.

The "story ladder" (state → complication → attempt → worse complication → reversal → payoff) is one reliable spine. The In My World skeleton is another. A one-joke sketch might need just setup, escalation and turn. **Every beat must change something**: the stakes, the information, the relationship or the joke.

## Escalation

Raise one thing each beat: scale (one house, then the town), cost, absurdity, or proximity (the threat gets closer). Rule of three works, but vary it: break the pattern on the third, or skip to a fourth that's absurd.

## Setup and payoff

Plant one specific, visible detail early, a prop, a habit or a throwaway line, and make the turn depend on it. The best payoffs reframe what the viewer already saw. A plain "secret power saves the day" twist leans towards flops in our In My World data (TEST).

## Perspective

Rotate who tells it:
- the outcast narrator (common);
- the bully;
- a bystander;
- an object or creature (the slug, the blaster, the arena);
- a deadpan third person;
- "you".

Our cast gives distinct voices for free (see voice-and-dialogue.md).

## Endings

Ironic button · reframe of the opening line · sequel stinger · a real two-option question · a loop back to the first frame (TEST: replays) · a cliffhanger only with an honest outlet. One ending, not two stacked CTAs.

## Length and pace (references, not rules)

Length is whatever the payoff needs. For In My World, the genre runs at about 210 wpm and most TOP posts are 60–170 s. Those are conventions, not advantages. A sketch can be 20 seconds, and a slow, deadpan read can be the joke if it's a choice. Spoken word budget at genre pace: 60 s ≈ 210 words.
