# Marketing fit: the game inside the entertainment

Entertainment first, but every script should do at least one job for Slugshot. The best scripts make the game the reason the joke or story exists, not an ad attached to an unrelated story.

## Pick the job (one main, maybe one secondary)

| Job | What it looks like in a script |
|---|---|
| **Character attachment** | Viewers come to like a specific slug (Floppy's dignity, Aquabeek's one-liners) and want to see them again |
| **Creature desirability** | A slug's look, personality or ability makes viewers want it as *their* partner |
| **World recognition** | Glowing caverns, partners chosen at hatching, slugslingers: recurring world details viewers start to recognise |
| **Competitive excitement** | Rivalries, underdog wins, a showdown with real (verified) gameplay |
| **Gameplay curiosity** | A real mechanic shown in a way that makes viewers wonder how it plays (only what game truth allows) |
| **Community** | Recurring jokes, characters and questions viewers can join in on |

## How the game should appear

- **Integral (preferred):** the premise only works in our world, e.g. a story about who gets picked as a partner, or the starter slug nobody wants.
- **Cast-led:** a universal premise performed by our slugs and slugslingers, so the characters carry the brand.
- **Light touch:** a story that stands alone, with slugs as recognisable background or a closing beat.

## Calls to action

- **Not every video needs one.** Never "like and subscribe".
- When one fits, keep it tiny, after the payoff, true to launch state (no "play now" before release), and tied to the story (a real question, or "follow to see who Floppy picks").
- Never break the story mid-way for a promo read. In our data, game promos that were part of the story didn't hurt reach. Interruptions are the risk.

## Truth

Fiction is labelled. Gameplay claims are checked. Our Floppy is the weakest slug, not useless (7 damage). Invented abilities are story, not features.
