# Captions, emphasis and visual hierarchy

## Captions

- Captions exist so the video works muted. Every spoken line gets one. **(A: TikTok recommends captions; research PDF clarity gate)**
- Spelling of game names is non-negotiable: the glossary fixes most, and the low-confidence list from `ss caption` shows what to check by hand. **(research PDF: "wrong game names")**
- **Phrase vs word-by-word.** Word highlight (PVP, PROMO, CHOICE presets) suits fast, energetic narration. Phrase captions (WORLD, SKIT, AISCENE) suit story narration where reading whole thoughts matters. **(D)**
- One to three words per caption page for fast lines; up to about six for slow story lines. Break at natural phrase boundaries, never between a name and its verb. **(D)**
- Captions disappear on text cards (automatic) and anywhere the screen already shows the same words. Never show the same words twice. **(D)**
- But a card must then show **the whole spoken line**. A card reading "In my world..." over the spoken line "In my world, everyone gets one slug" leaves a muted viewer without the rule. Either put the full line on the card, or set `"captions": true` on that scene and keep the card text short and different. *(Found in the first toolkit review, 2026-11-24.)* **(D)**

## Emphasis (`"emphasis"` in edit files)

Emphasise the word that carries the new information in that line:
- **Names** the viewer must learn: Floppy, Inferno (recognition is a goal; the research PDF wants viewers to "get the joke because Floppy appears").
- **Stakes and reversals**: "missed", "one shot", "but", "so".
- **The payoff word**: "hops", "dream".

Don't emphasise:
- more than one or two words per caption page;
- function words, or every noun;
- words in a line that's already the visual punchline. Let the picture carry it.

(All **D**: test emphasis density as an experiment if it matters.)

## Visual hierarchy (one focal point at a time)

Order of importance on screen: **the action** > hook (first seconds only) > captions > label > end card text.
- The hook box sits at the top and disappears after about 2.5 s. If the hook and the first caption say the same thing, drop one of them.
- Only one moving text element at a time. If the hook animates in, captions wait or stay still.
- Accent colour = "look here". Use it for emphasis words and the hook only. Each preset has one accent; don't add more colours.
- Labels ("Fictional story", "AI-generated scene") stay small and constant. They inform; they shouldn't compete.
- Read the frame sheet (`ss sheet`) as a thumbnail strip: if you can't tell what each frame is about in half a second, the hierarchy is wrong.
