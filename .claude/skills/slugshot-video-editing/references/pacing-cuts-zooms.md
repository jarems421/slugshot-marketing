# Pacing, cuts, zooms and transitions

## Pacing

- Pace follows information, not a clock. Cut when the viewer has taken in what the shot is for. A shot that only repeats what the last one said is dead time. **(D, consistent with the research PDF)**
- Fast is not the same as good: random interruptions make a piece harder to follow. **(research PDF; A: TikTok frames devices as optional)**
- Front-load. The first 1-3 s carry the premise; trim run-ups, menus, loading and "hey guys". **(A/C)**
- Vary rhythm: a run of quick cuts into one held shot makes the held shot feel important. Use the hold for the payoff or the reaction. **(D)**
- When retention data arrives (YouTube "stayed to watch", average view duration/percentage, Instagram retention), the drop-off point tells you which beat to cut or move. That data outranks these rules. **(A: platforms expose it for this reason)**

## Motivated cuts

Cut:
- on action (mid-shot, mid-jump, mid-impact), so the motion carries across the cut;
- the instant the information is delivered (the hit lands, the reaction shows);
- to the consequence after a cause ("I missed" -> his free lane);
- to a reaction after a surprise (friend's face, chat, or the slug's idle).

Don't cut:
- during the payoff itself;
- to restate what was just shown;
- just because N seconds passed.

## Zooms and punch-ins (`"punch_at"`, `"zoom"` in edit files)

- **Punch-in** (a quick snap closer, 3 frames, then hold): on the moment of consequence: the hit, the transformation, the miss, the face. One or two per video; more and they stop meaning anything. **(D)**
- **Slow push** (`zoom` 1.05-1.15 over the scene): stills, tension before a reveal, an emotional line. Not on fast gameplay, where it fights the action. **(D)**
- Never zoom so far that the slug, projectile or HUD element the beat is about leaves the frame. Check on a frame sheet. **(research PDF: keep critical action in the vertical crop)**
- In crop mode, the frame is already about 3x tighter than the 16:9 source. Decide crop vs blur per clip: crop when the action is central and the HUD isn't needed; blur when the whole arena or UI matters. **(D)**

## Transitions

- The default is a hard cut. **(D)**
- `"transition": "fade"` (fade from black) only for a change of time, place or reality (into a dream, an AI scene, "the next day"). It tells the viewer "something different now". **(D)**
- No spinning, glitch or swipe transitions as decoration; they read as template content and cost clarity. **(D)**

## Gameplay visibility on phones

- At phone size, the slug and projectile must be identifiable in one glance. If you have to pause to see it, punch in, crop, or choose another moment. **(research PDF: watch the vertical crop on a phone)**
- Keep text off the action: hook at top, captions in the lower-middle (69% down), labels small at top-left. The bottom ~25% and right edge are under platform buttons. **(A/C: safe areas, approximate)**
- Dark underground scenes lose detail after compression. Prefer brighter moments when choosing between two equal takes. **(D)**
